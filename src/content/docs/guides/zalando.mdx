<Hook title="Why Zalando's guide?">

Zalando, Europe's big online fashion platform, runs hundreds of engineering teams and thousands of APIs. In 2015 they published their API guidelines as open source, and they've been maintained in public ever since. It's one of the most complete, opinionated and _practical_ API style guides you can read, which is why it's the backbone of this course.

</Hook>

## Philosophy in four ideas

1. **API first**: design and review the contract before implementing it.
2. **API as a product**: treat consumers as customers; care about their experience.
3. **Consistency at scale**: hundreds of teams, one look and feel. Rules are enforced with a linter ([Zally](https://github.com/zalando/zally)).
4. **Compatibility over versioning**: evolve without breaking, version only as a last resort.

## How to read the rules

Each rule has a number and a strength, following RFC 2119:

| Level      | Meaning                                                                   |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **MUST**   | Required. Violations need a very good reason (and are flagged in review). |
| **SHOULD** | Strongly recommended. Deviate only with a documented reason.              |
| **MAY**    | Optional. A tool in your toolbox.                                         |

## Some distinctly Zalando choices

Not every rule is universal. These are the places where Zalando is more opinionated than most:

- **No URL versioning** at all; media type versioning when unavoidable.
- **`snake_case`** everywhere in JSON and query parameters.
- **`X-Flow-ID`** as the company-wide correlation header.
- **Problem JSON** as the only error format.
- Full **events chapter**, reflecting their event bus, Nakadi.
